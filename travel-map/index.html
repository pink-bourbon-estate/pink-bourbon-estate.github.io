Travel Map

Keep trips made of steps, see the countries you have been to, and highlight the actual roads you travelled. Everything stays in your browser, and nothing contacts another site unless you press a button and confirm.

Work in progress. Think of Polarsteps as the model: trips, steps, stats. The roads layer is a first version and uses a simple matching method, so treat the results as a starting point, not a survey.

Drag to move, scroll or use the buttons to zoom. Visited countries are shaded.

Roads you have travelled

Get road data, then match your route to it. Roads you travelled are drawn in the accent colour; the rest are grey. No internet or files needed to try it: the practice area is a made-up street grid.

Steps

    Where the roads come from

    Short answer: OpenStreetMap for the main map, government data as an extra. OpenStreetMap (OSM) is worldwide and is the only open source that also has footpaths, tracks, cycleways and bridleways. It is free to use under the Open Database Licence (ODbL): you must credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors", and a map made from it carries that credit. Ordnance Survey\'s free OS Open Roads covers Great Britain only and is roads only (no paths); it is a free download from the OS Data Hub, updated twice a year. Check its current licence wording on the OS site before reusing it (it is believed to be the Open Government Licence, which I have not verified here).

    How "all the roads" gets in. Three routes, from most private to least: (1) a roads file you load yourself, which never leaves your device; (2) a button that asks OpenStreetMap\'s Overpass service for the roads in a box around your trip (it shows the exact query first, and is capped to about 65 km across); (3) later, small downloadable regions made ahead of time. Whole countries do not fit on a free static site (the site limit is about 1 GB), so the box approach is how a free tool stays free. The Overpass main server asks for light use (under about 10,000 queries a day), so this page asks once per press and nothing more.

    What God\'s Eye View does. It is a similar idea: it takes roads from OpenStreetMap, routes with the OSRM service, and can use your own Overpass server. I read most but not all of its description, so I describe it only in outline.

    Honest limits. The matcher snaps each point of your route to the nearest road within the tolerance, prefers staying on the same road, and ignores roads with fewer than three matching points. It can mistake parallel roads and struggles with poor GPS. A proper map matcher (for example Valhalla\'s Meili) would do better and is a possible next step. Routing between steps uses the free public OSRM servers, whose web addresses I have not been able to test from here.

    Not built yet: a Google Maps Timeline import, receipts and card statements, choosing an area to download as a vector, shape or 3D file, tree data, run and interrail planning, and weather and flight layers.
