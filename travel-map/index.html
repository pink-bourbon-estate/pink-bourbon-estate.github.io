Travel Map

Keep trips made of steps, see the countries you have been to, and highlight the actual roads you travelled. Everything stays in your browser, and nothing contacts another site unless you press a button and confirm.

Work in progress. Think of Polarsteps as the model: trips, steps, stats. The roads layer is a first version and uses a simple matching method, so treat the results as a starting point, not a survey.

Drag to move, scroll or use the buttons to zoom. Visited countries are shaded.

Steps

    Where the roads come from

    The short version. The street map is a local copy of open road data that is rebuilt on this site, so your visit never asks anyone else\'s server for roads. For Great Britain the roads come from Ordnance Survey\'s OS Open Roads, which is free (published under the Open Government Licence) and covers every road for vehicles, with names and road numbers. Footpaths, tracks, cycleways and bridleways are not in it, so those come from OpenStreetMap, the only open source that has them (ODbL licence, credit required).

    How the copy is made. A job on GitHub runs each month while the site is built. It downloads the data, cuts it into small files (tiles) at three levels of detail (motorways and A roads when zoomed out, then B roads, then every road when zoomed in), and publishes them with the site. The code is in .github/scripts/build_roads.py. OS Open Roads itself is refreshed by Ordnance Survey twice a year (April and October), so most months the copy simply stays as it is; the monthly run keeps it current without anyone remembering. OS Open USRN, the street reference numbers, is refreshed every month but it is identifiers only, so it is not needed for the map.

    Why not one big file? The national OS Open Roads download is about a gigabyte, and a free static site holds about a gigabyte in total. Small tiles that are read only where you look keep the site light and quick.

    Outside Great Britain. OS data covers Great Britain only. Elsewhere there is the optional "ask OpenStreetMap directly" button (with a confirm step). More regions could be added to the monthly build from OpenStreetMap extracts.

    Honest limits. The monthly job has not run against the real data yet, so until it has, the page says the tiles are not built. The matcher snaps each point of your route to the nearest road within the tolerance, prefers staying on the same road, and ignores roads with fewer than three matching points. It can mistake parallel roads and struggles with poor GPS. Routing between steps uses the free public OSRM servers, whose addresses I have not been able to test from here.

    Not built yet: a Google Maps Timeline import, receipts and card statements, choosing an area to download as a vector, shape or 3D file, tree data, run and interrail planning, and weather and flight layers.
