Keeping a record of small radio before it disappears

Small radio stations are where local bands get their first plays, and when a station closes that record usually goes with it.

Open the Independent Archive Read the code

Why keep a record

Most new music in Britain is heard first on small stations. A band from down the road gets its first play on a volunteer-run community station, a pub-basement online station or a student station, long before any national playlist notices. Those stations are where local scenes meet: DJs learn there, promoters find acts there, and listeners discover the people playing in their own town.

That history is fragile. Commercial stations and the BBC keep playlists and logs. Small stations usually do not keep public ones, or keep them only on their own website. When a station closes, its website lapses and its history goes with it: which bands it supported, who played on which show, and when a sound first reached the airwaves. Nobody else holds a copy.

Stations are closing

Since 2022 one UK independent station after another has closed or cut back, nearly always because of rent, energy bills and running costs. In Bristol, SWU.FM closed in 2022 (it was later bought by Rinse FM) and 1020 Radio closed in 2023 after eight years. Liverpool's Melodic Distraction closed in 2023 after more than 9,000 shows. Worldwide FM moved to a much smaller service. Vandelay Radio in Swansea paused in 2024. Coast Radio in Peterhead closed in 2025.

Others are still on air but have had to ask listeners for help: Reprezent and Brixton Radio in south London, Threads and Loose.FM, Clyde Built Radio in Glasgow, and Resonance FM. Government support through the Community Radio Fund is only open to Ofcom-licensed stations, so online-only stations, which are many of those listed here, cannot apply for it.

Why the small artists matter

A play on a small station is often the only public trace that an emerging artist was heard on the radio at all. Those plays are evidence of a career beginning, of a scene existing, and of who championed whom. Kept together, they let anyone answer questions that are otherwise lost: which local bands got played this month, and where; which stations backed an artist years before anyone else; what a city's underground actually sounded like in a given year.

What this archive does

  • Lists independent and community stations across the UK, with what is known about whether each is on air and how much at risk it is.
  • Collects what each station plays, from what it already publishes, gently and openly, and keeps it in plain files anyone can download.
  • Stores every artist once, so a band played on three stations is one entry, not three.
  • Keeps the record even after a station goes quiet.

It is unofficial and non-commercial, carries no ads and no tracking, and every station can ask for its data to be changed or removed, which will be done promptly. If you run a station, or know one that is missing, please get in touch.
